Ingredients
Base & Aromatics
Bitter leaves: 500 g fresh or frozen pre-washed bitter leaves (can substitute with washed spinach or kale).

Raw skinless peanuts (groundnuts): 2 cups (400 g).

Onions: 2 large (1 for blending, 1 sliced for garnishing).

Garlic: 5–6 cloves.

Ginger: 1-inch piece.

Ground dried crayfish: ½ cup.

Vegetable oil: ¾ cup.

Bouillon cubes: 2–3 cubes (Maggi Crevette works great).

Salt & pepper: To taste.

Habanero/Scotch Bonnet pepper: 1 (optional, for heat).

Proteins
Beef or Goat meat: 500 g, cut into bite-sized pieces.

Smoked fish or Stockfish: 200 g (soaked and cleaned).

Fresh Shrimp/Prawns: 300 g (peeled and deveined).

Instructions
1. Prepare the Bitter Leaves
If raw: Boil the leaves in water with a pinch of baking soda to soften them. Drain and wash vigorously under cold running water, squeezing out the green liquid multiple times until the bitterness is reduced to your liking.

Squeeze out excess water and set the leaves aside.

2. Cook the Proteins
In a pot, combine the beef, half a chopped onion, salt, black pepper, and 1 bouillon cube. Cover with water and simmer until tender.

Add the soaked smoked fish or stockfish during the last 15 minutes of cooking.

Keep the meat and fish in the broth (reserve about 2–3 cups of broth).

3. Blend the Peanuts
Boil the raw skinless peanuts in water for 10 minutes, then drain.

Transfer the peanuts to a blender with 1 large onion, garlic, ginger, and the optional habanero pepper.

Blend with a small amount of water into a thick, smooth paste.

4. Combine the Stew
Pour the peanut paste directly into the pot with the meats and broth. Stir well over medium heat.

Add the ground crayfish and remaining bouillon cubes. Stir continuously for 10–15 minutes as it simmers so the bottom doesn't scorch.

Add the prepared bitter leaves and stir well to mix them into the peanut paste.

Reduce heat to low and let it simmer gently for 10 minutes. Adjust salt to taste.

5. Sauté the Shrimp Garnish
Heat the vegetable oil in a skillet over medium heat.

Add the fresh shrimp and sliced onion. Season with a pinch of salt and sauté until the shrimp are pink and the onions are soft.

Pour the hot oil, shrimp, and onions directly over the pot of Ndolé.

Serving Suggestions
Ndolé is traditionally served warm with:

Bobolo or Miondo (fermented cassava sticks)

Boiled plantains (ripe or unripe)

Boiled yams or white rice
